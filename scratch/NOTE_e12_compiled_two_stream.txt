E12: the stock bf16_both compiled path CANNOT be pipelined across two CUDA streams
2026-09-25 03:00. Found by the §6 equivalence check before any timing was taken.

## What was measured

    condition                        pipelined vs serial actions
    eager, pipelined                 BITWISE IDENTICAL (6/6, max|d| = 0.000e+00)
    compiled bf16_both, serial       correct (consecutive frames differ by ~5, as they must)
    compiled bf16_both, pipelined    CORRUPTED, non-deterministically:
                                       run 1  max|d| = 1.273
                                       run 2  max|d| = NaN   (all frames NaN)

Same code both runs; only a diagnostic print was added between them. **The corruption is
not deterministic**, which makes it a race rather than a mis-wiring.

The negative control detected a deliberately stale cache in 5/5 applicable frames in both
paths, so the check has power in the compiled path too -- the compiled pipeline is failing a
test that works.

## What it is NOT

* **Not late cloning into static graph buffers.** That was the first hypothesis: bf16_both
  graphs denoise_step under reduce-overhead, so its outputs live in static buffers, and
  run_pipeline clones at the end of the loop. The diagnostic reports
  `shared_tensor_objects=False` and per-frame values of NaN rather than a repeated last
  frame. Hypothesis refuted by measurement.
* **Not the model or the schedule.** Compiled SERIAL is correct, and EAGER PIPELINED is
  bitwise identical to serial. The two-stream schedule introduces no numerical difference
  of its own; it is specifically interleaving the GRAPHED callables across two streams.
* **Not something torch reports.** TORCH_LOGS=cudagraphs emitted no re-record, fallback or
  warning line. It fails silently.

## Why this was predictable, and was predicted

docs/E12_corun/README.md §7: "torch.compile's CUDA-graph trees were designed for one
sequential call stream. Interleaving the graphed embed_image and denoise_step across two
streams may trigger re-recording, fallback to non-graph execution, output-overwrite errors,
or hidden synchronisation." The rung graphs pg.embed_image@reduce-overhead+clone on the
prefix side and model.denoise_step@reduce-overhead on the expert side, so both stages are
replaying graphs from one shared CUDAGraphTrees pool while running on different streams.

## What follows

The spec prescribes the remedy in the same paragraph: "If the stock path cannot run
pipelined, capturing the same compiled functions yourself (torch.cuda.CUDAGraph, separate
memory pools) is an acceptable labelled harness change -- same kernels, same equivalence
check, isolated stages re-measured through it."

So:
1. **EAGER Step 1 is measurable now** and its equivalence is already verified bitwise. It
   answers KerColle's question for the eager configuration, with an in-session r of its own.
2. The **compiled** baseline needs the own-CUDAGraph harness before any of its numbers may
   be reported. Until then no compiled co-run number exists, and that is stated rather than
   filled with the stock path's output.
3. **This is itself a result worth reporting**: on JetPack 6.2 / CUDA 12.6 / torch 2.8, the
   stock torch.compile reduce-overhead path cannot be safely pipelined across two streams,
   and it corrupts silently rather than raising. Anyone proposing "just use two streams" as
   the cheap answer to co-run contention has to deal with that first.

## Do not

Do not report timings from the stock compiled pipeline. They run, they produce plausible
per-frame milliseconds, and the actions are NaN.
