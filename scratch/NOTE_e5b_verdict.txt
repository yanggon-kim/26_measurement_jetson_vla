E5b verdict: NO configuration meets §7's memory-latency-bound threshold
2026-09-24 23:10. Corrects a claim I made from the max-across-kernels figure.

## The claim I got wrong

I reported "long_scoreboard dominates every configuration, rising to 52-73 % in the compiled
ones -- the memory-bound signature". That used the MAXIMUM long_scoreboard share across the
kernels in each configuration. Taking a maximum is exactly what a threshold on the weighted
figure exists to prevent, and §7's rule is "long scoreboard >= 60 % means memory-latency
bound" applied to the phase, not to its most favourable kernel.

Weighted by kernel duration, with launch-bound kernels excluded per §1:

    config          time-wtd long_sb   max across kernels   §7 >= 60 %?
    pi0.5  best            47.6 %            74.9 %             no
    pi0    best            46.3 %            77.0 %             no
    SmolVLA best           30.8 %            57.5 %             no
    pi0    eager           32.4 %            44.0 %             no
    SmolVLA eager          30.0 %            41.5 %             no
    pi0.5  eager           19.5 %            36.6 %             no

NONE of the six meets the threshold. The action expert's kernels are NOT memory-latency
bound by the document's own criterion. That is a negative result for the PIM thesis and the
second one tonight, after the GPU-idle hypothesis.

## What IS true, aggregated by instruction class

Summing §7's classes (time-weighted, launch-bound excluded):

    config          LSU     control  ALU/XU   top three reasons
    pi0    best     67.2 %   16.1 %   10.7 %  long_scoreboard 46, barrier 16, lg_throttle 12
    pi0.5  best     48.4 %    7.2 %    9.1 %  long_scoreboard 48, imc_miss 12, short_sb 11
    SmolVLA eager   41.1 %   23.5 %   20.6 %  long_scoreboard 30, wait 21, barrier 14
    SmolVLA best    35.0 %   20.0 %   31.2 %  wait 31, long_scoreboard 31, barrier 10
    pi0    eager    34.5 %   16.3 %   44.9 %  wait 45, long_scoreboard 34, dispatch_stall 14
    pi0.5  eager    27.2 %    5.5 %   12.2 %  imc_miss 27, long_scoreboard 19, wait 12

Three things worth stating, none of which is "memory-bound everywhere":

1. **Only pi0 compiled is memory-subsystem dominated**, at 67.2 % LSU. It is the single best
   case for a memory-side accelerator in the whole set.
2. **pi0 EAGER is ALU/XU dominated**, 44.9 %, driven by `wait` at 45 % -- fixed-latency
   operations (transcendental, integer divide, FP64), not memory. Measuring the eager
   configuration and concluding "memory-bound" would be wrong for the very model the study
   started from.
3. **Compiling moves most models toward LSU** (pi0 34.5 -> 67.2, pi0.5 27.2 -> 48.4) but not
   SmolVLA (41.1 -> 35.0). So "optimise, then the memory wall appears" is a tendency, not a
   rule.

`imc_miss` is pi0.5 eager's largest single wait at 27 % and §7 has no entry for it, so its
cause is left open rather than guessed. C1 independently found the eager expert
imc_miss-limited in all three LeRobot models, so this is a reproduced observation and a real
question for the research side: what does imc_miss mean on this device?

## Method notes

* Launch-bound kernels are EXCLUDED from every figure above, per §1. They are present in the
  captures because the ncu regex matches short names that are substrings of one another, and
  they are flagged launch_bound_s1 in stall_attribution.csv.
* `selected` is excluded from the waiting denominator, per §7 -- it means the warp was ready.
* pi0.5/best is the re-capture taken with a `triton_` family regex after the stored target
  named a kernel that run never launched; its 17 kernels are the Triton set that
  --compile-mode reduce-overhead actually generates.
